# Design Constraints
## Spinlog: Marcus Rowe, Owen Mueller

### Social
 The social topical area fits into our project because it aims to directly improve vinyl collectors and listeners quality of life. Since our problem is addressing a lack of resources available to vinyl collectors, our solution's viability will be heavily determined by ease of use and helpfulness to the user. Each user should be able to keep track of at least 1000 records.

### Economic
Economics factors into our project for both the vinyl collectors and project team. For the project team we will be using personal funds for all development, for vinyl collectors they would need to purchase NFC tags for the first phase, and possibly a piece of hardware to replace the NFC tags. A trade off for vinyl collectors would be social vs economic. NFC tags are more annoying to use than a dedicated piece of hardware and don’t give as much data, but they would probably be cheaper, where a piece of custom hardware would be more expensive but much easier to use and give better data. We are opting for the NFC tags for now, and will evaluate the viability and ease of use once we have a prototype available for testing. We aim to make it less than $20. 

 ### Security
Security factors into our project because we will be collecting data from vinyl collectors. We would be collecting data about their collection like which records they own and how much they’re worth, and about the listening habits like what records they listen to, when they listen to them, and what their favorites are. All user data will be encrypted in transit with TLS 1.2 or higher.
